## The decisions

### Negative results commit as closed evidence records, not abandoned branches — *model-behavior-as-design*

Every card that fails its pre-registered keep rule — F-0j, F-0k, C-9, SC-1 (K2) — merges anyway. The evaluation report and metrics.json commit alongside the negative verdict, so the gap is permanently documented and the box-set state is unambiguous for every downstream card. This is not a consolation — it is the mechanism by which the sprint's evidence chain remains coherent.

Tags: `pre-registered-evals`, `evidence-governed`, `negative-results`

### Pre-registration commits before first full-set run, not before design — *model-behavior-as-design*

Every card's keep rules, thresholds, and spot-check conditions are committed in a separate commit before any full measured run executes. This prevents the most common form of evaluation fraud: adjusting the threshold to fit the result. The commit hash is recorded in the PR body as the pre-registration evidence anchor, making the sequencing auditable without trusting the author's word. Keep rules K1, K2, and K3 are card-specific: K1 covers detection recall or row coverage, K2 covers agreement quality against a shuffle baseline, and K3 covers yardstick wrong-accept rate staying within a pre-committed interval. Each card's PR body defines which rules apply and what the thresholds are.

Tags: `pre-registered-evals`, `evidence-governed`

### RB-1 as a checkpoint, not a correction: re-baselining six cards at once after geometry stabilises — *tooling-for-iteration*

Rather than re-running downstream cards ad hoc every time an upstream geometry card lands, the system batches them into a single RB-1 re-baseline pass once F-0i's partition and NB-3's on-lip adjustment are both stable. The key invariant: --box-set pre-rb1 swaps in a frozen snapshot of the pre-RB-1 box set at build time, reproducing every card's original published figures hermetially — a future upstream geometry change cannot silently drift the result.

Tags: `evidence-governed`, `pre-registered-evals`

### TS-0 label ledger: machine labels flagged by class strength, never filtered before training — *tooling-for-iteration*

The 472 strong-class labels (readers-agree on distinctive titles with reader text from at least two readers) are flagged in the ledger dataset but not removed. The 17 labels with third-reader dissent are similarly flagged, not silently dropped. The strong-class boundary is defined in the ledger schema — not in rb1.ts — and is fully reproducible from the export. No training run has been authorized; that gate is an explicit owner decision, not a function of label count.

Tags: `label-ledger`, `evidence-governed`

### SC-1 K2 failure reveals that ISFDB's single-genre catalog makes section-agreement shuffle-equivalent — *model-behavior-as-design*

SC-1's shelf-context reader achieves strong row coverage (K1) but fails K2 because ISFDB is a science fiction and fantasy catalog — even nonfiction rows mostly hold genre-tagged works, so section-agreement against a shuffle baseline is structurally near-chance. This is a catalog scope constraint, not a reader quality failure, and the card documents it explicitly. No sanity check against a multi-genre subset was run; the diagnosis is by inference from the failure pattern and the catalog's known scope. K1 passing confirms the reader works mechanically; it has no predictive value for K2 quality in this catalog configuration. No card is yet queued to re-test section-agreement against a broader catalog.
