## The decisions

### Dossier→presence seam: private engagement repo, sanitized public artifacts — *security-as-emergent-ux*

The texas-rr-engagement map lives in a private dossier repo; the FDE operating-presence map (#319) is the only surface allowed to publish artifacts from it. Per-artifact clearance is enforced at charting time, not at publish time — each artifact is ruled public or private before it is created, not after. Mechanically: the wayfinder template includes a clearance field in artifact frontmatter; the pre-commit lint checks for the field's presence and fails on any staged artifact marked private or uncleared. A cold reader opening texas-rr-engagement.md can identify clearance state from the frontmatter of each artifact entry before touching it. This prevents the failure mode where a useful artifact is published with client-identifying detail that was added incrementally and never reviewed as a whole. The alternative — a publish-gate review — was rejected because it moves the review to a moment when the artifact already exists and social pressure to ship it is highest. Charting-time clearance has no such pressure.

### Absolute public-git lint for the employer-evidence boundary — *security-as-emergent-ux*

The adr:employer-evidence-boundary pattern (commit e153189) uses a gitignored denylist of protected terms and a lint step that fails if any term appears in files staged for public-git commit. The article describes the protected terms and their category — never reproduces them. The cycle-2 FDE audit scrub was precautionary, not a caught near-miss — which means the lint has not yet been validated by a real violation. That gap matters: a lint that has never caught a real violation is a lint whose failure path is untested. The /validate suggested action (run the lint against decisions/wayfinder/texas-rr-engagement.md after the #356 sanitization pass) is the next opportunity to exercise that path against real content. On tooling choice: a gitignored denylist was chosen over gitleaks/trufflehog because the protected terms are not secrets in the cryptographic sense — they are policy-defined proper nouns. Secrets scanners pattern-match on entropy and known credential formats; they would not catch a client name.
