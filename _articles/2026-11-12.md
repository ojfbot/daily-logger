### Pre-registration locks F-0 sampling density before execution: scope reduction ruling committed to prereg.md as an amendment — *model-behavior-as-design*

The F-0 run on sf-store-001 sampled every 3rd frame (205 of 616) rather than every frame or every 30th frame. The every-30 pilot projected 29.8 hours against a 10-hour card limit — a resource constraint on the evaluation session — triggering a scope reduction ruling before any step ran. That ruling was committed as an amendment to evals/F0-frames/prereg.md before execution began. The trade-off of every-3 sampling is that inter-frame coverage is sparser than a full run, but the pre-registered protocol explicitly accepts this in exchange for staying within the card limit; the agreement metric (label distribution match against pilot-000 caches) is robust to stride at this density. What the pre-registration discipline buys: it makes the sampling decision irrevocable before results are visible, eliminating the temptation to adjust density post-hoc when results look unexpected. The protocol is reproducible across sessions because the stride is committed, not chosen after the fact. F-0 machine agreement is complete as a gate; TS-0 and TS-2 can proceed. F-0 is not a blocking prerequisite for TS-0 — both can advance in parallel — but having clean F-0 agreement against pilot-000 removes one class of environment-drift uncertainty before the training-set scaffold stages begin.

### Off-by-one in trial-count boundary: verification and admission gates must use separate named constants — *tooling-for-iteration*

PR #97 fixed a boundary error where the sixth physical trial's verification step checked against the admission limit (five prior trials) rather than the completion limit (six completed trials). The admission gate rejects a candidate with six prior trials to prevent running a seventh; the verification gate needs to accept six completed trials as the success condition. Sharing the constant between both checks makes the final trial permanently unverifiable regardless of output quality. The error was caught by the failing trial output — the sixth trial's clean run was rejected by verification, making the logical conflict immediately visible. The fix introduces a separate named constant for the completion limit used only at the verification gate, so anyone adding a seventh trial type later encounters a named boundary rather than a reused literal.
