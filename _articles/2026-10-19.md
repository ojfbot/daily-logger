## The decisions

### Combining duplicate workflow `env` blocks rather than adding a third — *tooling-for-iteration*

The YAML repair deleted the redundant `env:` header and its blank line rather than patching around the duplicate or restructuring the file. Strict js-yaml parsing rejects files with two top-level keys of the same name at the first duplicate occurrence — adding `ACTION_REVIEW_DISABLED` to the existing mapping is the structurally correct fix and leaves no surface for future writers to misread the file as intentionally split. The alternative — appending a third mapping or commenting out the duplicate — would have produced a file that parses correctly today and confuses the next maintainer immediately.

### Capping new action identities at two per article lifecycle with evidence requirements — *model-behavior-as-design*

The verified action registry introduced in #381 caps new suggestion identities at two per article lifecycle and requires factual evidence, goal evidence, and an independent verifier for each. The action registry is the ledger that tracks AI-generated suggested actions across the pipeline's two-agent loop (a proposer agent and a separate verifier agent). Without this cap the registry grows unboundedly with every article pass, making the token-aware budget in issue #386 impossible to respect and duplicate detection unreliable. The tradeoff is real but bounded: legitimate suggestions beyond the cap are held (not dropped) and carried into the next article cycle. At the time of this writing, the cap has not yet been hit in practice — the 138 held suggestions predate the cap and were grandfathered in. The failure mode of an unbounded registry is not theoretical: issue #386 measures the system already pressing against its context ceiling.

### Rebase-onto-merged-article as the repair sequencing pattern — *tooling-for-iteration*

The workflow YAML fix (db69911) was rebased onto the already-merged October 18 article correction (f94b11a) before being merged via #388. This sequencing ensures the repair never races the correction — a parallel merge would risk the repair landing on a stale base and silently undoing the correction's YAML state. The rebase-only pattern is now documented in c88039f as a recoverable procedure. If you need to repeat this: rebase the fix branch onto the tip of main after the correction merges, force-push, then open the repair PR against main.
