Wednesday split attention between two systems that don't look related on the surface — an Anthropic feed watcher in morning-cockpit and a self-improving quiz harness in core — but share the same underlying philosophy: the agent should practice skills on real data rather than waiting for a human to hand it a task. The clearest proof that this is working: the /merge-quiz harness, built to teach the agent about merging, ran its own teach-then-test loop and found four defects in itself. Both systems shipped.

## What shipped

### morning-cockpit ([#40](https://github.com/ojfbot/morning-cockpit/pull/40))

PR [#40](https://github.com/ojfbot/morning-cockpit/pull/40) merged: Anthropic watch Stage 1. Watches Anthropic's technical output directly via verified feeds (a hardcoded allowlist of Anthropic-owned domains with RSS/Atom endpoints, enforced at ingest time), maintains a ledger, and applies the local-first multi-dimensional relevance rubric introduced in ADR-0017. That rubric scores each item across several dimensions — recency, topic overlap with active harness contexts, and source authority — and routes items to the ledger with a score rather than filtering them out, so the four harnesses (voi-interview, revision-forecast, and companions) work from ranked signal rather than raw volume. Previously, those harnesses waited for the creator ecosystem to repackage Anthropic's output; Stage 1 removes that intermediary layer entirely.

## The decisions

### morning-cockpit watches Anthropic directly, not through the creator ecosystem — *assistant-centric*

Rather than ingesting newsletters or YouTube summaries of Anthropic's output, Stage 1 wires verified feeds — a hardcoded allowlist of Anthropic-owned domain endpoints, checked at ingest — directly into the watch ledger. ADR-0017's relevance rubric then scores each item across recency, topic overlap with active harness contexts, and source authority, routing scored items to the ledger rather than filtering them outright. One fewer transformation layer means fewer opportunities for repackaging to dilute or delay context-sensitive material. The rubric has not yet been externally reviewed; that validation is deferred to Stage 2 scoping.

### /merge-quiz teaches before it tests — *tooling-for-iteration*

The original /merge-quiz design tested the agent's merge knowledge immediately. The corrected design (06619cd) runs a teach-then-test loop: present the concept, then assess. A quiz that only tests without context narrows the signal to recognition rather than building durable understanding. The self-test pass on the new design found four defects: two factual errors in quiz content, one structural flaw in the test loop's branching logic, and one coverage gap for a common merge conflict pattern. The harness finding flaws in itself on day one is the validation signal the design needed.

Tags: `core`, `skill-catalog`, `agentic-harness`

### /blind-sweep consensus ranking was inverted — ordering fixed — *model-behavior-as-design*

In the original implementation, answers appearing across multiple independent runs (consensus) were ranked lower than answers appearing only once (singletons). With the inverted ordering, callers would observe singletons surfaced as top results while consensus answers were buried — confident-looking output that was systematically wrong. The fix in 7dc53d8 corrects the value ordering: consensus beats singleton. The generalizable lesson: any ranking function aggregating multi-run outputs needs an explicit ordering test before it's trusted. This pattern belongs in an ADR, not just a daily log, because the next harness author will repeat it otherwise.

Tags: `core`, `skill-catalog`, `hardening`

### k=3 in /blind-sweep is a stopping point, not a floor — *tooling-for-iteration*

The documentation update in f7f07b6 clarifies that running three sweeps is where the harness stops, not a minimum below which results are unreliable. Stopping points and floors are different contracts. A caller who interprets k=3 as a floor will continue running sweeps looking for convergence that the design doesn't promise — wasting compute and accumulating false confidence in outputs that may not be stable. The distinction needs to be named explicitly at the call site, not inferred from implementation.

Tags: `core`, `skill-catalog`

### /merge-quiz deposits what it learned into the selfco vault — *assistant-centric*

The selfco vault is the personal Obsidian knowledge store managed by the selfco-box daemon — it ingests Notion, iOS, and MCP captures via a path-jailed Claude agent and surfaces connections through the /vault cultivate pass. Commit 3904fd2 adds a vault-deposit step at the end of the /merge-quiz loop: the agent writes a structured note capturing what was reviewed and what the quiz revealed. The note schema includes the skill name, session timestamp, concepts covered, and defects found. This closes the retention loop: practice without deposit is noise; the /vault cultivate pass uses deposited notes as a serendipity surface to connect quiz outputs to other vault clusters. Without the deposit step, /merge-quiz's findings — including the four defects — would have no durable record.
