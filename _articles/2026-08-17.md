## The decisions

### Wayfinder as a defect-detection mechanism, not just a planning tool — *model-behavior-as-design*

The phantom `newline-ai-course` path was caught not by a test or CI check, but by a wayfinder ruling (ADR-0107, `core/adrs/ADR-0107.md`) that had already classified that repo as out-of-scope for the fleet. Operationally, a wayfinder ruling is a structured markdown decision record in the `core` repo — authored by a human or model session, tagged by scope, and surfaced during structured review when a model or reviewer checks a candidate change against the registry. It is not automated tooling; it depends on the review process consulting the registry. That makes it a weaker guarantee than a compiler check, but a stronger guarantee than undocumented tribal knowledge: when the ruling exists before the code is written, it makes the mismatch legible rather than invisible. The alternative — no ruling, no registry — means the phantom path could have survived into Lab 1 template work, silently causing cold-start failures. The honest boundary of the claim: this is a human-in-the-loop lint, not an automated one. Its reliability scales with the consistency of the review process that surfaces it.
